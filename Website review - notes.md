## 1. Positioning & messaging

- Core tension: the site needs to say **what we do** *and* **why it matters** (the true
  value), not just describe a service. Right now it leans too far toward describing the
  offering.
- **"More than a health check"** is the anchor idea — but it currently reads as a service
  line rather than a value line. It's really about *why Evida exists*, so it may belong in
  an **About** section.
- We're caught between two registers: the **pitch-deck voice** and the **marketing voice**.
  Need to pick the marketing voice for the public site.
- Everyone in the space dances around the same wording ("stop guessing, start doing" —
  cf. MyFitnessPal and similar). We want to say it *better*, not just differently. Some of
  these are adjacent products, not direct competitors.
- The message that resonates internally ("more than just a health check") doesn't
  automatically land with outsiders (customers, investors). Reference points: recent
  Bhutan-raise feedback _(unclear)_.

### Key message to feature
- **"No single data point tells the whole story."**
  - Use as a standalone pull-quote / banner section (magazine style, part-width, possibly
    a carousel), interspersed through the page rather than a dedicated CTA block.
  - Could pair with quotes sourced from reputable news outlets — cite (and link) the
    source for credibility and potential SEO backlinks.
- Core value prop to keep surfacing: **we bring your data together in one place and pair it
  with the right advice** (the "single source of truth" / single data piece idea).
- **Membership organising framework: Data → Insight → Action** (three pillars). Use this
  triad consistently on both the Home summary and the Membership page.

### 30-second elevator pitch (working version)
> Preventative healthcare that gives you a baseline health check, plus a platform that
> integrates your wearable data with your medical history and presents it to your GP.

- Lead with **"preventative"** / prevention before cure.
- Lifestyle-medicine angle, especially for **young people** — worth a dedicated phrase.
  Phrase to explore: **"baby reps"** (younger-audience framing).
- The goal is to **enhance** existing care, not replace it: you keep seeing the same GP who
  knows your history, facilities, and lifestyle.

## 2. Homepage structure & flow

Current site feels disjointed because the layout keeps switching rhythm (two-column hero →
one big image → three columns → vertical, etc.).

### Proposed flow
1. **Hero**
2. **Membership** (the "what" — already exists)
3. **How it works**
4. Supporting sections (data insights, blog, about) leading down to…
5. **Membership / pricing** again at the bottom as the closing CTA.

### Layout principle
- Adopt an **alternating two-column layout** (text/image, then image/text) like competitor
  sites, instead of the current one-big-image-then-three-blocks pattern.
- Keep a **consistent background feel** across sections so the page reads as one continuous
  page. **Competitor reference: Too Healthy** — everything on one page, consistent
  background across sections, and no anxiety about content duplication.
- Open discussion: how to reconcile the recurring **"three-item" blocks** with a two-column
  approach — don't want a jarring vertical break each time.
- Use **anchor links** so section summaries jump to the fuller page/detail.
- Reduce visual noise; too many stacked blocks makes it feel busy.

## 3. "How it works" vs "Membership" (page consolidation)

- Considering **merging "How it works" and "Membership"** into fewer pages (broader goal:
  reduce total page count).
- **Argument for keeping "How it works" separate:** SEO / AI-search value. "How does X
  work?" is a common query, and a dedicated page maps cleanly to it. Keep it in the sitemap.
- Compromise: on the homepage, have a **summary** of how it works with an anchor/CTA into
  the detailed page.
- Possible reordering: swap the emphasis so **Membership = the action/state** and
  **How it works = the explanation**, with a short summary living on the homepage.
- If keeping a 3-item format: intro on one side, 4–5–6 supporting points on the other;
  distil the journey into **three** clear steps.

---

## 4. The "journey" / first six months

- Current "your first six months" is **5–6 bullet points — too much to process**, and the
  points aren't all at the same level.
- **Group into three stages** (whiteboard labels + Granola):
  1. **Initial** (initial test)
  2. **Grounding / 6 months** (baseline understanding)
  3. **Ongoing** (ongoing monitoring)
- Per the whiteboard, the six steps can survive as **2 sub-steps per stage** rather than
  being cut — the win is grouping them under three clear headers, with pull-quote banners
  between stages.
- The initial blood test / biomarkers detail belongs here — add a concrete section on
  **what's actually collected in the blood test**.
- Ongoing monitoring: annual app-based follow-up; blood pack sent out. Asterisk/footnote
  for bespoke scans and blood packs.

## 5. Pricing presentation

- Headline framing: **~£27/month**.
- Actually billed **annually up front** (**£320/year** — confirmed in the June 29 copy
  session); the true monthly figure is **£26.67** (£26.6666…), so £27 is the more
  palatable round number. _(The "328" in the July 3 transcript was a mistranscription.)_
- Displaying a monthly price while billing annually is common practice, but be careful it
  doesn't read as disingenuous ("just £27/month" while charging the full year upfront).
- Consider alternative framings:
  - **Daily** — e.g. **~65p/day** (competitor uses this).
  - **Quarterly** as a middle option (caveat: people don't naturally think in quarters).
- Open question: what's the real tension we're managing — the number itself vs. the
  perception of how it's billed.

## 6. Navigation, CTAs & pages

- **Reduce the number of CTAs ("owls").** Too many options → decision paralysis (paradox of
  choice). Pick a clear **primary CTA** and prioritise it.
- Place the **primary CTA on the right** in paired CTA layouts.
- Keep a **persistent top-right login / sign-up** so the main conversion action is always
  available no matter where the user is on the page.
- Consider a small inline link rather than a full button in some spots.
- **Contact:** can live in the **footer** (often where these sites put location/contact
  info). No firm right/wrong on placement.
- Add a proper **sitemap** and a **Help page** (also helps AI/search understand the site).

## 7. Blog / content

- Blog is pulled from **Substack**. Options:
  - Surface it as a **section** on the site rather than a separate nav item.
  - Use the **search page** as an entry point into the blog (search opens the search page;
    a nice low-friction way to get eyeballs on content without adding another nav item).
- Article flow: reader opens an article on-site (ideally doesn't force them out to
  Substack), then returns / continues into the funnel.
- Keep a **CTA on articles** (e.g. subscribe / get started); Substack handles the
  newsletter subscription.
- Note: blog feed may not be pulling all posts live — check the Substack connection /
  publish state.

## 8. Imagery & media

- **Replace the stock "white lady doctor standing and smiling"** image — it reads as
  generic/AI-generated and not human. Move that background/style elsewhere or drop it.
- Prefer **active** imagery over passive stock.
- **Avoid the cliché "lady running" hero video.** Appreciate competitors who skip video
  entirely; only add video if it genuinely earns its place.
