## Health starts with how we live

The way you live each day has more power over your health than most people realise.

Most of the conditions that we treat every day as GPs are **preventable** — and this is the focus of lifestyle medicine. Up to 80% of conditions managed in primary care — including high blood pressure, heart disease, diabetes, obesity, autoimmune conditions, and many mental health concerns — are linked to lifestyle factors.(1)

Lifestyle medicine addresses the **root causes of disease**, rather than simply managing symptoms. It works with the body's underlying physiology to improve health outcomes — often with fewer side effects than medication alone. Medication remains integral to healthcare, but the impact of lifestyle change is frequently underestimated despite growing evidence of its effectiveness. Even small, sustained improvements in daily habits can enhance energy, mood, and long-term health, while bigger transformations can slow disease progression and, in some cases, **reverse the condition entirely**. I have observed these benefits and positive outcomes not only in my work as a GP, but also in my personal experience as a patient.

Better health isn't about quick fixes — it is about reclaiming control in a sustainable way, **one step at a time**.(2)

## Origins of lifestyle medicine

**Lifestyle medicine is not new.** The connection between daily habits and health was well known as far back as 2,000 years ago, with Greek physician and philosopher Hippocrates famously saying *"Let your food be your medicine, and your medicine be your food."*

**Modern lifestyle medicine is new** (relatively speaking). The medicinal power of improving our daily lives was left forgotten until 1990 when the first lifestyle medicine textbook of modern times was published. Over the subsequent three decades, lifestyle medicine has evolved into a **robust, evidence-based discipline**, resulting in the American College of Lifestyle Medicine being founded in 2004 and British Society of Lifestyle Medicine being established in 2016,(3) with both centred on the "6-pillar" framework.

## The six pillars that could change your life

Lifestyle medicine is grounded in **six interconnected pillars** — nutrition, movement, sleep, stress, substance avoidance and connection. Research consistently shows that even modest, consistent improvements in these nutrition, activity, sleep, stress, and social connection can reduce the risk of chronic disease and improve quality of life.(4) This protects our future health while unlocking energy, vitality, and wellbeing in the present. We'll explore each of these in more detail in future articles.

## Living well, not just living longer

At its heart, lifestyle medicine is simple: **medicine through the way we live.** It is inherently personal — shaped by individual routines, environments, and priorities, with lifestyle prescriptions tailored accordingly. Despite its simplicity, these changes have the potential to be transformative and add life to your years, not merely years to your life.

Every intentional choice we make — what we eat, how we move, how we rest, how we connect — creates a ripple effect of health, happiness, and longevity.(5) This incredible potential for improving health is why I feel so passionate about lifestyle medicine and chose to specialise in it.
